The Bench

Tested on the bench. Graded in the open.

Every platform grades condition in adjectives. We grade it on the bench, on camera, against a standard anyone can read — the same standard, every lot, no exceptions.

1

Run it

The tool works under real load — not idling for the camera. Batteries are cycled; motors are loaded; meters are read against a reference.

2

Show the serial

The number goes on camera and on the lot’s Chain of Hands card, checked against theft records before the lot can run.

3

Name the flaws

Every defect the bench finds is spoken out loud and written on the lot. What the bench can’t verify, the lot says it can’t verify.

Grade A

Work-Ready

Runs to specification today. Put it on the truck in the morning; it earns by noon.

Grade B

Bench-Worn

Honest wear from honest work. Runs true; every flaw found is named on the lot and priced into it.

Grade C

Project

Sold as a project and priced like one — for the hands that enjoy bringing a good tool back.

The Counter

Advice, over the counter.

Every trade has a counter where the real knowledge changes hands. This is ours — short answers, no sales pitch, free to anyone who walks up.

Q · 1

“How do I know a used battery isn’t lying?”

Watch it work, not idle. On the Bench, packs are cycled under load on camera — a battery that sags in the first minute tells on itself. And anywhere you buy: if the seller won’t run it loaded, walk.

Q · 2

“What is my old kit actually worth?”

Whole and honest beats parted and polished. A complete kit with its story — receipts, serials, the work it did — sells to the next pair of hands for more than its pieces fetch from strangers. Appraisal here is free; bring the story with it.

Q · 3

“First year in the trade — what do I buy first?”

The bag, not the truck. A tested starter kit, a meter you trust, and boots that fit will out-earn a shiny box of debt. Then buy the upgrade cycle: pros sell good gear every time they level up.

Q · 5

“I’m an apprentice. Is there anyone to just… ask?”

Yes — the Apprentice Hour. Before featured shows, working masters take questions live: about the trade, and about the lots about to sell. No question is too first-year. It is the highest-status hour on the Rail, because here, teaching is.

Q · 6

“Who actually answers when I write to the mart?”

A person — the owner reads every note himself and answers the way a good counterman does: by name, quickly, and like your time matters. Because on this platform the counter is staffed, not scripted.

Q · 4

“A tool I bought used just locked itself. Now what?”

Connected tools can be locked by a previous owner — it happens to honest buyers every week. Prevention is the serial, shown before you pay, and a bill of sale with that number on it. Here, both are standard on every serialized lot.

About

Why “altruistic”?

Most markets ask only, “what’s it worth?” We also ask, “who does it help?” Almus Mercatus is being built around generosity as a feature, not an afterthought.

We’re just getting started. The first shows, sellers, and listings are being prepared now — and the door opens when they’re ready to be proud of.

The mart is being built for the trades first — electricians, plumbers, welders, mechanics, and the apprentices coming up behind them — because nobody else is building for them at all. No platform tests a used tool live, checks its serial before it sells, or honors a forty-year kit the way it deserves. That is the gap. That is the market.
