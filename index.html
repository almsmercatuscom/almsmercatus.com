How it works

Simple, on purpose.

1

Watch

Sellers host their shows live. You see the tool run, its serial number, and the giving — all before the first bid. Two minutes tells you what a listing page never can.

2

Bid fair

Rounds run on a fair-close clock. Late bids extend the round, so lots are won by wanting them, not by gaming the last second.

3

See the giving

Every lot states its giving up front, in plain words. When a sale closes, you know exactly what it did.

Giving

“The giving built into each sale will be stated plainly, so you know where it goes.”

That is the whole policy, and it will not grow fine print. The precise model is being set now — and when it is chosen, it will be printed here in one sentence. When the giving begins, it begins with the trades: tools in apprentices’ hands, through the programs that already do that work well.

Support the mart

Fuel for the mission.

The mart runs on its sales — but gifts move it faster, and every gift is thanked by name, or kept anonymous if you would rather. Purchases and donations will be handled by Stripe: card, Apple Pay, or bank. The mart never sees your card number, and neither do we store it.

The payment switch is being wired now and opens with the first shows. Until then, the surest way to move this forward is a note — we answer every one.

About

Why “altruistic”?

Most markets ask only, “what’s it worth?” We also ask, “who does it help?” Almus Mercatus is being built around generosity as a feature, not an afterthought.

We’re just getting started. The first shows, sellers, and listings are being prepared now — and the door opens when they’re ready to be proud of.

The mart is being built for the trades first — electricians, plumbers, welders, mechanics, and the apprentices coming up behind them — because nobody else is building for them at all. No platform tests a used tool live, checks its serial before it sells, or honors a forty-year kit the way it deserves. That is the gap. That is the market.
